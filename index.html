Latest Project

Self-starter or sidekick? Independent and spillover effects of Opportunity Zones on housing supply in cities

Opportunity Zones (OZs) were created by the 2017 Tax Cuts & Jobs Act (TCJA) and offer investors tax breaks for investing in “low income areas”. We used quarterly USPS vacancy data from 2015-2022 to understand the effect of OZs on housing production. We found that while housing production did increase in OZs post-designation, about 41% of that growth gets absorbed when spatial autocorrelation is accounted for (spillover from nearby areas). We did not find any evidence that clustered OZs contribute to growth. Additionally, housing production was unevenly distributed; relatively advantaged neighborhoods saw the largest gains while distressed OZ Census Tracts saw negligible impacts. Published in Environment and Planning A: Economy and Space in October 2026!
