Latest Project

Self-starter or sidekick? Independent and spillover effects of Opportunity Zones on housing supply in cities

Opportunity Zones, created by the 2017 tax law, give investors tax breaks for putting money into low-income areas. Critics say they pay for development that would have happened anyway. Using USPS address data from 2015–2022, we found that Opportunity Zones in principal cities saw more housing growth after designation. About 41% of that growth came from spillover from nearby areas, not from the program itself. Clusters of zones didn’t help and sometimes hurt. Better-off neighborhoods gained the most, while the poorest areas saw almost nothing, which suggests the program is falling short on equity. Published in Environment and Planning A: Economy and Space in October 2026!
